    Today, we're combining Cowork and Chat into one Claude. You can fire off questions and then turn to serious work in the same conversation. When your computer is open, Claude can use your local files and apps - and if you close it, Claude will keep working with its own computer in the cloud.     The model is a little better at most things, but the change I expect people to feel first is cost: Fable 5.1 solves agentic coding tasks at roughly half the cost of Fable 5. On CursorBench, it's state of the art at max effort while costing a little over half of what Fable 5 did. I also like its writing. It sounds a lot more natural and a lot less "Claude-y" than our previous models, and it (imho) follows style instructions more reliably. The other thing I'd watch is science. Everyone has been talking about math for a few weeks, and I think other disciplines will see similar breakthroughs soon. On Terminal-Bench-Science, a Stanford-led benchmark with tasks written by working scientists, Fable 5.1 scores 52.6%, more than double Fable 5 (24.7%) and Opus 5 (29.0%). We're not at a country of geniuses in a datacenter yet, but we sure are making a lot of progress.

    We're introducing Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1. They're the world's most advanced models for coding and knowledge work. Fable 5.1 excels at complex, long-running tasks, and its research capabilities offer an early glimpse of how AI models will contribute to scientific progress. Across our benchmarks, it sets a new standard, and at lower effort levels it can achieve similar or better results than Fable 5 at a much lower cost. Fable 5.1 also takes steps to address feedback we've heard on price, data retention, and safeguards. Cache reads with Fable 5.1 cost 75% less than Fable 5's. This reduces the cost of the model in practice by around 25% for typical workloads and up to 45% for highly agentic ones. Our cybersecurity safeguards now flag benign requests about 60% less often, and on basic biology and medical questions, we've recently reduced the fallback rate by around 85%. We're also introducing Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS), which give enterprise customers complete privacy (the same as zero data retention) while still being state-of-the-art at preventing adversarial use. Data stays on the customer's own cloud, not Anthropic's. We developed EFS with more than 100 customers, and it rolls out in phases, starting this fall: https://lnkd.in/emfmnazm Claude Fable 5.1 is available everywhere today. Claude Mythos 5.1, our model for cyberdefenders and life scientists, is available through trusted access programs. Read more: https://lnkd.in/eYNbsf3S

    I sent Claude three screenshots and five words. It built me a virtual room. ⠀ This week Felix Rieseberg, who builds Claude at Anthropic, shared his new homepage made with Opus 5.5. A rendered desk, a retro computer, rain on the window. It stopped my scroll. ⠀ So I screenshotted his post and typed: "Connect to Blender and create." ⠀ Here is what Claude Opus 5.5 did next: ⠀ 1. Set up Blender in the cloud and modeled the whole room in Python. A walnut desk, a beige 80s computer, a green lamp, my three books, a kopi o kosong, and Marina Bay on a rainy night outside the window. 2. Rendered it, studied its own renders and fixed what looked off. It even caught a lamp reflection in the glass that looked like a moon. 3. Turned the renders into a website. Click the computer and you get a working green screen terminal. 4. Deployed it to Vercel. ⠀ I never opened Blender. ⠀ Come sit at my desk: https://lnkd.in/e9nvjJzB ⠀ Type ABOUT, WORK or BOOKS. Switch off the lamp. Tap the window for lightning. Turn the sound on for rain and a 50 Hz hum, the frequency of Singapore's power grid. ⠀ Three things I took away: ⠀ 1. Show, don't spec. Three screenshots beat a two page brief. 2. Tools turn a chat into a build. Blender, a browser and Vercel did the heavy lifting. 3. The new skill is direction. I gave the brief. Claude did the build. ⠀ Thank you Felix Rieseberg for the inspiration. ⠀ What would your homepage room look like?
